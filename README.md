# **A Factory-Management Game Inspired By Cell Biology**

### 1\) Overview 

You take control of a newborn cell that doesn’t seem to have a parent; in fact, it  seems to have appeared out of thin air, into a random environment. At first you’re  able to propel yourself gently, allowing basic movement control.  

Your membrane is totally barebones, only being able to filter materials based on  size (a hard-wired property determined by the physical attributes of the  membrane). It allows small materials to pass through, which interact with  metabolic pathways and are converted into energy. This energy is then sent off  to fuel necessary biological functions, preserving the life of the cell. 

### 2\) Energetic Cells 

Fats, carbohydrates, and proteins are the main types of machines managed by  the player, corresponding to storage, transportation, and processing respectively.  Each one performs certain biological functions, and it is the player’s mission to  use them optimally in order to survive and grow stronger within the game world.

At the start of a session, you begin as a single cell with the ability to move around  and collect certain materials. By default, some materials produce energy when  manipulated by metabolic pathways. Over time it becomes necessary to manage  the materials and machines within a cell, and so the player is incentivized to  develop pathways.  

### 3\) Automated Pathways

A pathway is a chain of machines within a larger network that feed into one  another and execute in sequence. Pathways are logical structures that help  streamline cell functions by triggering, promoting, or inducing specific internal  behaviors to guide material distribution and organization. Without pathways, cells  become entirely probabilistic because interactions between materials/machines  within the cell are driven by random collisions. 

The player gains the ability to control the statistical distribution of materials with  the use of pathways, improving overall efficiency and thus making the player  stronger.

### 4\) Membrane Machines 

Certain materials cannot pass through the membrane automatically. A membrane  machine acts specifically on materials right at the boundary of a cell, both  internal and external. They allow passage of specific materials into/out of the cell  by consuming energy to override natural resistance. 

Membrane machines can also act as external appendages, enabling additional  control over the immediate environment. Many of these machines exist, such as a  tail that helps the cell move, or a sensor that informs the cell of external stimuli.  

The overarching theme of all membrane machines is of course that they interface  with the outside world. 

### 5\) Cell Optimization

Inside the cell are machines which are responsible for processing, manipulating  and storing materials, as well as the transportation of materials between  machines/storage units. All machines require energy to function. The instantaneous fitness of a cell, i.e.  the cell’s current performance rating, is found by adding up the total cost of  machinery plus the metabolic rate, then subtracting it from the total energy of the  cell. 

__*INSTANTANEOUS FITNESS*: fitness \= energy \- (machine \+ metabolic)__

These constraints emphasize the need to optimize cell efficiency in order to  survive, as a lack of energy leads to the shutdown of metabolic pathways which  are necessary to live. Therefore it is in the players best interest to not only avoid  wasting energy, but also to accumulate as much as possible while maintaining  overall performance.  

Concentration of materials naturally creates drag on a cell, and so it is also  beneficial to design lightweight pathways and systems for transporting unneeded  materials out of the cell.

### 6\) Multicellular Systems 

Considering the dynamics of a single cell, it is not hard to imagine ways in which  multiple cells might interact in some way. Special membrane machines act as  adhesive patches that allow other cells to connect.  

Connected cells have a direct line of communication that allows them to monitor and respond to one another’s behavior immediately. The downstream result of  having multiple connected cells is as follows: first, it creates bidirectional  communication that manifests as feedback influencing both participants over  time. Second, it glues together the pathways of cells such that their internal  systems become synchronized. 

Cells effectively act as modular components that can be fitted together  combinatorially, creating unbounded variations of dynamics that can be used to  perform specific tasks. On top of that, the exact combinations that are valid at  any given time are entirely determined by cells independently. That is, each cell  dynamically creates its own rules of interaction over time.
